# Echoself ICP

Every claim carries a tag. `verified` means evidenced. `assumed` means it is a
hypothesis. `dead` means tested and failed.

Read `ethics.md` before doing anything with this file.

## Who buys

- [verified, from the site] People wanting to leave a lasting legacy for loved
  ones, and families seeking to preserve the memories and wisdom of aging
  relatives.
- [assumed] Primary buyer is the adult child, aged 35 to 55, buying on behalf
  of a parent aged 70 plus. The parent is the user. The child is the payer.
- [assumed] A secondary buyer records for themselves, usually after a
  diagnosis or a milestone birthday.
- [assumed] The buyer is comfortable with technology. The user often is not,
  which makes onboarding the hardest part of the product.

## The trigger event

Handle this section carefully. The trigger is real and the marketing response
to it is constrained. See `ethics.md`.

- [assumed] A milestone. An eightieth birthday, a retirement, a new grandchild,
  a fiftieth anniversary. **This is the framing the product should lead with.**
- [assumed] A move into assisted living, or a house being cleared out.
- [assumed] A health event. Real, common, and not a marketing opportunity.
  Echoself should be findable at that moment, not promoted into it.

## What they say, in their own words

Placeholders until `growth-research` fills them from public, non-support-community

- [assumed] "I wish I had recorded my dad's voice."
- [assumed] "She tells the same five stories and I never wrote them down."

## The core product problem

- [assumed] Time to value is months, not minutes. An Echo is thin until enough
  recordings exist. This is the retention problem and no amount of distribution
  fixes it.
- [assumed] The daily prompt habit is the product. Growth work should sell the
  habit and treat the Echo as the payoff.

## The top objections

- [assumed] "This is creepy." Answered by the live demo, which is playable
  without signing up, and by never implying the Echo is the person.
- [assumed] "What happens to the recordings." Needs a plain, prominent answer
  on the site. Flagged as a proof gap.
- [assumed] "My dad will never use this." The real blocker. Onboarding a
  70-year-old is the product's hardest job.

## Who this is not for

- Anyone in acute grief. The product is for before, not after.
- Anyone looking to recreate a person who has already died.
  That is a different product and a worse one.
